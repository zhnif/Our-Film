The Beginning

"Every story has a first scene."

There's always a before and an after. A moment that doesn't announce itself—no music swells, no camera slowly pushes in. Just an ordinary day, ordinary light, and then you. And somehow, without knowing it, I stepped into a different version of my life.

I don't know exactly when it became clear to me. Maybe it was the way you laughed at something I said, not to be kind, but because you actually found it funny. Maybe it was the quiet—the kind of quiet that only exists between two people who don't need to fill every second. Maybe it was later. Maybe it had already happened before I noticed.

But I know this: there is a version of my days that existed before you were in them, and I don't particularly miss that version. Whatever it was that changed—I'm glad it did.

This is where our story starts. Or maybe this is just where I start telling it.

The Day I Remember Most

There are days that feel like years. And then there are the ones that last forever somehow—not because anything remarkable happened, but because something about the light, or the way you were there, pressed itself into me like a photograph developing slowly in a dark room.

I keep that day somewhere that doesn't have a name. I return to it sometimes when the world is too loud or the night is too long. I don't think I've ever told you that. I probably should have.

SCENE 03B
FRAME NOT FOUND

The One I Almost Missed

I almost didn't pay attention. I was somewhere else in my head—the way I sometimes am—when I looked up and there you were. Just being you. Not doing anything in particular. Just existing in a way that somehow made everything else stop mattering.

I don't know what I would have missed if I'd looked away. I'm glad I didn't. I'm glad for every small second I chose to actually be there with you instead of wherever else my mind was trying to go.

SCENE 03C

The Late Night That Became Morning

Some conversations have no ending. They just slowly dissolve into silence, and then into sleep, and then into a kind of closeness that doesn't need words anymore. That's what I think of when I think of us at our best: the way time becomes elastic, unhurried, generous.

I've had long nights before. But not like those. Not with the feeling that the night itself was on our side, giving us more minutes than it gives anyone else.

SCENE 05 · THINGS I NEVER SAY ENOUGH
"THANK YOU."
"FOR STAYING."
"FOR CHOOSING THIS."
"FOR EXISTING."
"FOR BEING YOU."
— a letter —

I don't say this as often as I should, and I think you probably know that, but I want to say it here, in writing, so it exists somewhere outside of me:

You are genuinely good. Not in the polished, effortful way that some people perform goodness. In the real way. The way that shows up without being asked, that notices the small things, that doesn't make a scene about kindness.

You have made me better. Not by telling me to be better—but just by being someone I wanted to show up for. That's the kind of influence that changes people quietly, at the root level, without announcement.

I notice more than I say. I appreciate more than I express. And I know that's something I should work on. But I want you to know—even when I'm quiet, even when I get absorbed in whatever is happening in my own head—I see you. I feel it. I'm grateful for it.

Thank you for being patient with me. For being curious about me. For all the times you stayed when it would have been easier to go.

I'm glad you stayed.

SCENE 06 · YOUR BIRTHDAY

Happy Birthday,

FRAME NOT FOUND

Today is yours. Entirely, completely, undeniably yours.

I've been thinking about what I want to say to you today, and I keep running into the same wall: the things that matter most are the hardest to put into language. Not because they're complicated—but because they're so large and so quiet at the same time, like something you can only ever point toward and hope the other person sees what you're pointing at.

So here's what I'll say: I am lucky. Not in the abstract, sentimental way people say that. In the specific, daily, ordinary way. Lucky that you exist. Lucky that our lives managed to intersect at whatever strange angle they did. Lucky that I get to know you—the real version of you, not the public-facing one.

I hope today is soft. I hope it gives you what you need, whether that's a celebration or a quiet room or something in between. I hope the year ahead holds something you didn't expect—something good, surprising, yours.

You deserve so much of the world. I know I say that, and I know everyone says that to people they love, but I mean it with the kind of specificity that doesn't translate well into sentences. You deserve to be known. You deserve to take up space. You deserve to be chosen, again and again, every ordinary day.

I choose you. On your birthday and every other day. With all the imperfect, genuine care I have.

— with everything,
